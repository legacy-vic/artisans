    Also known as Akir the Mighty

    A quiet and courageous man of about thirty-eight, found at the stables of Pa'iq, who means to protect Tanzu at all costs. He's sworn for life to Tanzu's father, who owns the stables, and to his family, and knows he'll never again be a free man.

    Appearance & Character

    Akir is quiet, and keeps his word. He knows Amesh isn't a good man: he trades in stock, and has sold people as well as animals (see Slavery). Akir values protecting Tanzu above anyone else, the father included.

    History

    The execution

    Around a decade before 211 ABU, Akir killed the man who sold his wife and child into slavery, in Jhansa. He was discovered almost immediately and taken prisoner by a couple of headhunters, who turned him in to their broker. He had a death warrant, and was to be marched to Neferopolis and hanged for murder, but his captors wanted to make money off him first. He was paraded around Jhansa as a man-killer, bound to other prisoners and slaves with chains and bronze collars, and made to march through town in a single-file line. There, he and several others were bought by a slaver, who marched them to Khadesh and put them up for auction. Tanzu's father, Amesh, the owner of the stables of Pa'iq, bought him for a small fortune. If he hadn't, Akir would have swung. Amesh was selling off his slaves then, buying tjarka and raising his stable, and he bought Akir to aid in its construction. Akir may have thought Amesh wanted to save him.

    The vow

    Feeling indebted to the man, Akir vowed to protect him and his family until his very last breath, knowing he'd never again be a free man. Only afterward did he learn that Amesh had sold people too. By 211 ABU, Amesh trades in people only on the side, seeing more profit in tjarka, and Akir is the only slave he keeps. Akir knows by then that he was bought to build the stable, not out of mercy. The vow was made on a lie, and he keeps it anyway, for Tanzu.
