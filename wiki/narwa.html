    Narwa

    Narwa

    Range
    Abu, Anklan Isles, Bali, Matau, Talvar

    Large mammal, with a sturdy build and tough hide. They have distinctive tusks that vary in number but are all dotted across its very large face. They're primarily used for protection and foraging for foods such as digging up roots, uprooting shrubbery, and tearing off the bark of trees. The color of their skin ranges from light grey to a mud brown. They inhabit the savannas of Athura, and have few natural predators due to their formidable tusks and imposing stature. They're exceptionally loyal and social creatures, often seen in small herds comprised of their immediate family, but many young male narwa leave these family units to roam the savannas and deserts alone, where they'll look for a mate from another herd to run off with, eventually forming their own herd.

    Narwa are domesticated by many villages for their dung, which they use for their crops, tusks, and hide. Despite this, within Abunali culture, it is only slaughtered when absolutely necessary, and some people consider it an extension of their family due to its extreme loyalty.
