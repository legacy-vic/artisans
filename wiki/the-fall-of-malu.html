    The Fall of Malu

    The dra'iq attack of 196 ABU that killed King Arabah, and made a king of the smuggler captain who drove it off, Alexium Ariadne. Malu was rebuilt, and renamed Phylomina.

    196 ABU

    For some time, the city of Malu was ruled by a dignified and honest man, King Arabah. One evening, the peace was disturbed. A rumble, followed by a crash emitted from the southern city wall. From the dust and debris flew the biggest creature the city’s populace had ever seen. The Dra'iq, known only from fable, had revealed itself to the city of Malu. The people watched in horror as it began to lay waste to the city. Too late to save the southern districts, the army began to utilize their defenses. Many trebuchets and arrows were fired at the dra’iq, but few hit it, and for every shot that was missed, civilian casualties grew. The dra’iq, growing more agitated, swept the city walls, burning each and every soldier firing upon it. After three devastating sweeps, it soon began flying toward Malu Palace in the center of the bay. The city could only watch as the end drew near. The King did the only thing he knew how to in the moment. He knelt before his very throne and began praying. The towers around the palace toppled as wings crashed into them, falling onto the palace, fissuring the roof. Debris and rubble fell on many noblemen inside. A stray stone slammed the king in the chest, fracturing his chest. As he lay against a wall, attempting to catch his breath, he began to accept his fate. As things go quiet, he hears the ripple of flames barreling past the palace. It doesn't sound like dra'iq flames, no—he watches as a flaming projectile scorches across the sky and narrowly misses the dra'iq as it soared overhead. In unison, the people of Malu felt hope reclaim their hearts. On the horizon, smuggler vessels sailed into the bay and fired upon the beast with everything they had. Having avoided the vast majority of projectiles, the dra'iq dives upon the small fleet, and with a final wave of projectiles, the dra’iq pulled away from its attack—it had been shot in the eye. With the dra’iq retreating, flying west into the mountains, King Arabah demanded he know the names of each and every one of his saviors, to which the remaining servants and noblemen began to exit the palace and retrieve them.

    Once the leading ships had docked at the palace, the smuggling crew rushed into the palace to aide the king, only to be met with the sad image of his defeat. The smuggler captain had approached, and the king, not expecting to see his long-time friend, Alexium Ariadne, began to smile. Unable to laugh without coughing up blood, King Arabah attempted to make small talk and reminisce. But Alexium, along with everyone else in the great hall, knew the end was nigh for the king. As he struggles to keep his eyes open, he mutters his final plea, that Alexium takes his place as ruler of Malu. Alexium's heart skips. He rose and looked upon the city as smoke billows from the many ruins. Shaking his head, he asks the king how he's expected to manage, but returning his gaze, he finds that the king is now dead, and those around him stand at the ready, awaiting Alexium's orders.

    The next day, Alexium’s crewmates reported a strange howl coming from the caverns beneath the palace. Curious, they investigate. It didn’t take long for them to discover the reason the beast attacked the city. An infant dra’iq lay emaciated on the jagged stone shore at the mouth of the cavern, its weak howls being amplified by the cave. Unsure of what to do, he thought: If the dra’iq returns for its baby, and discovers it, there may be little damage. But if the dra’iq returns and the baby is dead, it will finish what it started. With that, Alexium decided the best course of action would be to nurture the infant until it was capable of surviving on its own and then release it back into the wild. This, however, didn’t take long. A week passed, and the infant was as spry as could be. Though still dangerous, King Alexium had it relocated safely and quietly back into the matause jungles.

    A year later, Alexium had finally found it safe to bring his family to Malu. A decent portion of the city had been rebuilt, but the scars from the year before are still obvious. Despite this, Alexium decreed that the city shall go by a new name, representative of its restoration. Malu henceforth shall be known as Phylomina. Alexium’s wife, Aiore, made a hobby out of foraging from the local jungles and would often take their very young daughter, Vaenera. On one of these days, Aiore reaches into a bush and grabs something warm. Instantly reeling back from the odd sensation, she takes a closer look. It’s a young dra’iq, the same one from before only slightly bigger. Though, she wouldn’t know that. Vaenera, being no older than seven, asks to keep it. Aiore, having lived in Tabla Pise for most of her life was ignorant of the dra’iq and so she agrees. They stow the infant into the fruit basket while it slept, and carry it back to Malu Palace. Eventually, Alexium had found out, and was furious. Everything he had worked toward—his enterprise, the city, his family—with the infant in their possession, all of it was now forfeit. For a month they had kept it a secret, locked in a room, feeding it fish. Aiore apologizes profusely, trembling in fear for what she has done. Vaenera, hearing all of this for the first time, gazes at the dra'iq, intrigued. It’s just an animal, surely it wouldn’t misbehave if it didn’t have reason to. Alexium asks Aiore where they found it and she states it was just outside of the wall in the berry grove. Alexium, not liking the sound of a dra’iq making its home so close to the city, ponders. Most dra’iq don’t abandon their territories once its been marked, so even if released, it will just grow right outside of the city. 'By killing it right here, we can prevent future suffering...but should its mother were to return—I dare not ponder the thought.' Seeing no rational alternative, Alexium opts to safeguard it until further notice, sending word to High King Alfor Zuul—not about the young dra'iq, that would remain a secret, but for the armaments required to defend against such a threat.
