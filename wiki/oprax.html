    Oprax

    Oprax

    Show on the map

    Oprax sits in the fertile basin of southern Icos, once home to the Gurtai (see The Peoples of Old Icos). It shaped into a feudal government over generations, with several Ōkimi crowned at any given time, until they eventually crowned a Tennō to rule over the once-kings of the land.

    Oprax is now an empire, though its people don't directly call it such, as they don't use imperial terminology. Its empress is known as 'the Blood Empress' all across Artisans, but within Oprax, such a name results in death to those who speak it.

    Some Opraxi carry Gurtai blood. The names of Opraxi settlements speak to their origins or purposes.

    Honor

    Opraxi culture is extremely honor bound, so much so that lords will commit seppuku to preserve or restore honor, avoid capture, atone for offenses, or as a formal capital punishment. A northern lord wouldn't be let past the wall, at least not without being thrown in chains.

    Internal Struggle

    Present-day Oprax is a direct result of a centuries-long internal struggle. Its government changed shape in several ways, chief among them becoming less bureaucratic and more war-like, shifting toward hereditary clan leadership and the rule of warriors.

    It was a victory for the rebel clans beneath the Tennō, who wanted a less bureaucratic state. Many noble lineages were targeted by rival clans, pulled from their homes, and executed because of long-standing feuds that could never be settled diplomatically. The Tennō himself was merely a puppet to his advisor, who in effect held more power than the Tennō ever could. Unfortunately, the Tennō paid the price of being the figurehead. The martial clans assumed he was the leader of the opposition, and when he lost, he was executed by the rebel daimyo.

    The advisor attempted to play the daimyo's side, and for a while he did, ratting out old loyalists and bending the knee. But the daimyo was no fool. If the advisor had his strings in nearly every castle, he was a threat that needed to go.

    At his coronation, the new Tennō turned to the advisor and sentenced him to death for his crimes against the old Tennō. He cited treason, disloyalty to his liege, and dishonor to his clan, among other crimes. The advisor attempted to plead for forgiveness, but no man surrounding them faltered. The begging was an insult they had to bear. A man from a lesser clan, the Honda, stepped forward and handed the new Tennō his sword, with which he cut down the advisor and held the bloodied blade high. It was the start of a new age for Oprax, one where dishonor is met with steel and blood.

    The new Tennō raised a new castle further south on an island, Shinuhokori Fortress. The empress is his granddaughter.

    Many civilians, clans, and bureaucrats fled the chaos by several desperate methods. They built makeshift ladders to scale the walls, and even risked the harsher climates of the Kintari Mountains. They meticulously carved thin escape passages through the walls, covering them with thatch and leaves to avoid detection by nearby patrols. Lord Hiro Kuroshi was among the refugees of this era. The advisor had given his name to clan Honda, a rival with an old feud against his house, knowing it would come for him. Hiro succeeded in saving his family, and refused ever to return.

    The refugees weren't safe anywhere in Icos. Opraxi were targeted by stadians, many of whom had lost family in Anteo Zuul's fruitless war with Oprax, and they couldn't go anywhere near Oprax itself. The west was still open to them, but the hilltribes held prejudices of their own, and many refugees were looked at with hostility. Gideon and Cedric Talvo, with Hiro, brought refugees across the sea to Athura, so that they might live peacefully in new lands.

    Defenses

    Oprax is exceptionally easy to defend. A defensive wall spans around the entire basin, and the border of Oprax lines up with its perimeter. Both the western and northern mountain passes are quite narrow to squeeze armies and siege engines through, and the northern passes' paths run along steep drops into the rapids below. There's very little an offensive force can do to effectively penetrate these defenses, unless it uses naval warfare to attack from the south, which means sailing through The Blackmaw or the Abunala Sea. Both are particularly dangerous for a navy.

    Anteo's Failure

    Oprax refused to join the Bicontinental Union, and Anteo Zuul tried to force it. By the time he rose to the High Sovereignty, the wall was complete, and Oprax simply waited him out. The defeat was exceptionally embarrassing for the High King, and agitated him to no end.

    Worse still, his predecessor had a victory over Oprax that he did not. Ōnari and Kabejō Garrison were once Opraxi, but Oprax was unable to strategically supply the garrison for either settlement, and they were lost to Stade when Tarris Kalmere was still its ruler, before rising to High Sovereignty. That alone was enough to enrage Anteo.

    Raids & Navy

    Oprax has since grown confident, increasing its raids on stadian settlements, and even training captains and navigators to specifically maneuver through the Blackmaw. It doesn't have an entire navy by 211 ABU, but it has a decently capable naval fighting force.
