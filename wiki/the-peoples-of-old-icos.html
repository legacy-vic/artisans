    The Peoples of Old Icos

    All of Icos at one point in time was tribal.

    Long ago, when Azkassar was the largest realm in all of Artisans, the people of Icos knew only a few native realms, and none of them practiced feudalism. Neither did Athura. Azkassar was the first civilization in history to establish feudalism, and "king" was a title heard only there.

    The Ibyans

    Ibya was the first major power on Icos. It spanned what are now Pysos, Pavilia, Masseos, Erseos and Thena in the Ibyan Isles, along with most of the eastern coast and the minor islands around them, and it later founded a settlement on the mainland that became Acrenon. Modern icosi descend from them.

    The Ibyans knew nothing of kings, and they had no single ruler. Minor landholders, the strategoi, each governed a region, a nome, and the people who lived off its land. The strategoi were all of one culture, and they saw themselves as one realm: they traded with one another, dealt with one another, and needed one another. Community was paramount to them, so it was no surprise when they formed a senate of the governors, who listened to the people they served and made changes that would appeal to them, strengthening that sense of shared community.

    Some settlements grew into cities, and some cities grew over the domains of other landholders. When that happened, the city's governor would usually take the other governor in as a vassal, and in time became the holder of a city-state. The title didn't change, only the power behind it.

    In time, to compete with the other realms, Ibya had to reform its governance, centuries before 211 ABU. It took up the feudal ranks of its neighbours, and the greatest city-state holder, at Pavilia, was made Prince over the rest. The senate didn't survive it. The reform is what let Ibya join the Commonwealth of Stade as a principality rather than be swallowed.

    The Moirish

    Not a formal realm, but a collection of tribes in central Icos called the Moirish, a name that roughly means "people of the moorlands". Their land was Moirach. They were a shorter atlae, who could make their home upon crags and moors alike. The term 'moire', as in Linnenmoire and Endlemoire, comes from these moors, which the Crannochs still hold.

    The Morgari

    Not a formal realm, but a collection of tribes in the north called the Morgari. Their land was Morgai, now Morgar.

    The Proto-Opraxi

    Not a formal realm, but a collection of tribes in central Icos called the Oinu, the proto-Opraxi. Their land was Oinui. They lived as far north as where modern Endlemoire sits. They, alongside the Moirish, constantly fought the Ibyans, who sought to expand their reach further inland. Acrenon rose and fell several times during this era.

    The Gurtai

    Not a formal realm, but a collection of tribes in the south called the Gurtai. Their land was Guraxa.

    The Ror

    Not a formal realm, but a collection of tribes in the west called the Ror. Their land was Upror. They were shaggy atlae who grew about as tall as a groll.

    The Azkassari in Ibya

    When Azkassar fell, some of its noble families fled to Ibya, where they lived as refugees among people they considered beneath them. Many found the Ibyans' simple governance easy to understand, and grew wealthy as advisors, commanders, and even landholders themselves. Quietly, they gained influence and gathered soldiers.

    They knew they stood no chance of challenging Ibya so soon. So they went west instead, to build a feudal realm by subjugating weaker peoples.

    The Founding of Linnenmoire

    The Azkassari houses that had allied with one another founded a settlement west of Ibya: Linnenmoire. Its name is a subtle nod that not all of it was won by bloody conquest. They mingled with the local tribes, and likely married into some of them. It was Icos' first major city, and the beginning of feudalism on Icos.

    The Seat in the North

    In the north, a new seat was raised in imitation of the rulers down south, and was named Morgar. It took a long while to complete: a walled city atop a hill near the open plains, some miles south of where Ancrage sits today.

    The Petty Realms

    Some of the noble families who'd been granted holdings away from Linnenmoire began to expect its downfall before it came. Karlaine was among them, though its dynasty and nobles weren't of Azkassari descent, but a mixture of Ibyan and Moirish.

    After The Cremation of Linnenmoire, over the course of a century, the surviving rulers across Icos established their realms as petty kingdoms and princedoms, each claiming the primacy of their forebear, Linnenmoire. Karlaine grew into the largest city on Icos, as people migrated there and traffic increased. The rest were keeps that towns later grew around, or villages that ballooned in size. The seats along the major trade routes grew the most.

    Linnenmoire's ruins were a mixture of blackened glass, ash and stone. An Azkassari house rebuilt the once-great city under a new name, Endlemoire, and the blackened glass of Linnenmoire still decorates its grand structures today. Endlemoire never reached the prosperity Linnenmoire once had.

    The Commonwealth of Stade

    Faced with the threat of Morgar to the north, the Three Great Princes banded together and formed the Commonwealth of Stade, named for the high hill where they negotiated its creation. See Kingdom of Stade.

    The War for the Basin

    In the southern fertile basin, the proto-Opraxi warred with the Gurtai. They pressed south from where Ōnari now sits to Sekitō, then west past Nōka and south past Toyohara. Gurtai tribes burned between Nōka and Kumoori, between Zousen and Yanebori, and near Kohan Castle. Others fled west, past Mukaikumo Castle, Yamamura, Isomura and Biwako, and out through the western pass that Sekijō Castle now watches.

    This took a couple of centuries.

    The Shaping of Oprax

    Over the generations thereafter, Oprax shaped into a feudal government, with several Ōkimi crowned at any given time, until they eventually crowned a Tennō to rule over the once-kings of the land.
