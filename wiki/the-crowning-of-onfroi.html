    The Crowning of Onfroi

    The judgment in Karlaine by which Aethel Zuul refused to recognize Cedric Talvo as king, and made a king of his fifteen-year-old son, Onfroi. It took place the same year Anteo Zuul died.

    A Realm Without Legitimacy

    What began as a small hamlet on the shores of Athura had become something considerably harder to dismiss (see The Founding of Trion). Trion had developed into a political center. Novon had grown around its edges, made up of refugees from Oprax living under the protection and lordship of Lord Hiro Kuroshi, and at the cliff's peak stood Greystone, its limestone walls built for generations to live within. The region had cropland, livestock, mines, a growing harbor, and a people willing to recognize Talvo authority.

    What it didn't have was legitimacy. To the rulers of the Bicontinental Union, Cedric was the descendant of a disgraced house, and the old accusations of treason had never entirely disappeared (see Talvo Royal Family). A kingdom that existed only because its neighbors hadn't yet disputed it was vulnerable to any ruler powerful enough to do so.

    The Winter Road

    Cedric returned to Icos with Hiro, Onfroi, and a small delegation, with the appearance of a diplomatic mission and too few men to resemble a challenge. Rumor placed the High King not at High Sonham, but in Karlaine, at the court of Mandel Kalmere.

    Winter had settled over the continent. Roads disappeared beneath the snow, and the frigid air sent Onfroi into coughing fits, which he attempted to conceal whenever his father looked toward him. He'd known of his family's history, and of Wietmoire, from stories told in the safety of Trion. Now he passed banners of houses that had outlived his own family's ruin, and settlements whose rulers thought the Talvos exiles, pretenders, or curiosities from overseas.

    Another King's Throne

    When Cedric entered the royal hall, whispers followed him. "The Prince Overseas." "They dare call him a king?" The stadian nobles held the oldest resentments, while the Morgari and Uprorian courtiers were less openly contemptuous. He ignored them all. Then he saw the throne, and upon it sat Aethel.

    Mandel stood among his own courtiers, quick to smile when Aethel spoke, and yielding space without being asked. Cedric recognized sycophancy when he saw it. Aethel appeared to recognize it as well, and at moments his expression suggested irritation. Yet he went on sitting upon Mandel's throne. He might've disliked being flattered, but he didn't dislike being obeyed. Beside him stood his younger son, Alfor, watching.

    In the center of the hall, Cedric drew his sword. A guard's steel slid partway from its scabbard. Cedric paid him little attention, placed the sword upon the floor before him, and knelt. The two men had known one another years before, when neither held the position he now did, and Aethel didn't indulge it. "State your purpose."

    Cedric explained what had been built in southern Athura. He asked that the High Crown recognize Talvar as a kingdom within the Union, and then asked to be acknowledged as its king.

    Eight Kingdoms

    Aethel's eyes settled upon Onfroi, who stood beside Hiro, doing his best to remain composed. Then he looked back to Cedric.

    "And what will you do for me, brother?"

    "Forgive me, but is being ruler of eight kingdoms not enough?"

    Several nobles gasped. The Union had six. Cedric had counted Oprax, which refused it, and Talvar, which Aethel hadn't yet granted.

    Aethel's expression hardened only slightly. "I meant no offense by it. Our families' histories have been negatively connected for generations. I intend to mend that."

    The Daughter Refused

    Aethel asked the boy his name, considered the answer, and offered him his daughter, Aeleina. It would've bound the ruling house of the Union to the Talvos, and made Talvar kin to the High Sovereignty. For a moment, it was everything Cedric had crossed the sea to obtain. But he'd already given his word. Onfroi was betrothed to Hiro's granddaughter, Kota.

    "You married your son off to a lowborn? And an Opraxi at that."

    To many in the hall, the decision was incomprehensible. Hiro said nothing. He'd helped to preserve the Talvos when they had little left, and helped bring the refugees from whom Talvar had grown. Cedric had given his word before there were walls grand enough for foreign rulers to notice, and wouldn't break it now that keeping it was inconvenient. He affirmed the arrangement.

    The hostility he expected never came. Aethel faintly smiled.

    "I respect a man of honor more than a man of desperation."

    The Judgment

    Cedric wouldn't be recognized as King of Talvar. For an instant, the journey appeared to have failed. Then Aethel turned to Onfroi, and recognized him in his father's place.

    The decision astonished the court. It avoided elevating a man who carried the full weight of the Talvos' grievances, while acknowledging the realm he'd built. Onfroi had been a child when his family was killed, and had taken no part in the old dispute with the Kalmeres. His throne would also depend upon Cedric's administration for years.

    Cedric bowed his head and thanked Aethel. It wasn't the victory he'd requested, but Talvar had what it needed.

    The Truth

    Afterward, Aethel spoke with Cedric alone. He told him that Gideon hadn't knelt, and that he himself had stood there (see The Massacre at Wietmoire). He was the only Zuul who'd ever told Cedric the truth.

    Legacy

    The delegation left Karlaine with Cedric carrying no crown. His son did. For the first time since the fall of their ancestral city, the Talvos possessed a kingdom acknowledged by the highest authority in the Union. Cedric then took his son to see his mother, Lady Haileen Ermsburry.
