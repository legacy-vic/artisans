    The Fall of Talvar

    The destruction of Talvar by a dra'iq, and the ruin of the Talvo Royal Family that followed, before the Bicontinental Union was formed.

    A City on the Cliffs

    Talvar towered over the ocean, upon cliffs just beyond the steppe. By the time Oswulf Talvo founded it, the Talvos and the Crannochs had made peace with the hilltribes of the western frontier, hence why the hilltribes allowed the city to be built quietly, with no conflict. The Talvos had forged alliances with them, and were their kings (see Talvo Royal Family). With the city's defenses nearly unassailable and their influence growing, they were undeniably a threat to the ruling house of Stade, the Kalmeres. Diplomacy became their shield, as they offered trade, tribute, and goodwill to a crown that fretted over them. They'd also raised a second bastion, High Sonham, hidden deep in the mountains. It was a refuge the hilltribes asked for, and asked that nobody be told of. The Talvos kept the promise, even from Stade.

    The Charter

    The Talvos were no one's vassals. A king of Stade who couldn't hold the west, when the Talvos already did, had released them from fealty by charter in return for a quiet border.

    The Conspiracy

    The Zuuls, who wanted the west, found High Sonham under Aevor Zuul, and told the Kalmeres that only a rebel hides a fortress. The two houses entered into conspiracy with the rest of the realm, and waited for the moment Talvar might become vulnerable.

    The Dra'iq

    A colossal dra'iq descended upon Talvar with devastating force, burning the city and crashing into the ground as though it were a living meteorite. Half the city collapsed into the sea.

    The Charge

    Before the Talvos could recover from the chaos, Tarris Kalmere, then a young king, declared them guilty of treason, for raising High Sonham in hiding. It was the one true thing in the charge against them. A king can't betray a crown he doesn't serve, so to call it treason, the Kalmeres had to say the charter never existed, and Tarris burned their copy of it. Stade's claim to the west rests on that lie. Stadian forces swept across the steppe to capture the surviving family. The Zuuls, their rivals, moved as swiftly, laying claim to High Sonham and capturing any Talvos who slipped past the crown's men.

    The Knee

    Cornered, starving, and outnumbered, the last king in the west, Conrad Talvo, bowed to the crown and swore fealty to spare his bloodline from execution, despite the Zuuls' demands for their heads. He didn't ask the tribes first. His crown was annulled, and by that the west became the king of Stade's by law.

    Aftermath

    The Talvos returned to Stade humbled and stripped of their legacy, and later built Wietmoire Manor. The Zuuls were made Dukes of Tuvar. When the realms formed the Bicontinental Union, to put an end to the opportunism that followed every disaster of the dra'iq, the Talvos were excluded entirely, as their name still carried the stain of treason.

    The fall of Talvar, and the knee, gave the Kalmeres and Zuuls reason enough to war with the hilltribes, in an attempt to keep them weak. The Talvos' peace had been strengthening them. Had things never been discovered, the hilltribes could feasibly have become lords of a Talvo-led kingdom.

    The hilltribes remember the knee as clearly as they remember the granaries, and not all of them would welcome a Talvo back.

    The Talvos had a copy of the charter as well, and it was in High Sonham when the Zuuls took it. It's lost: the Zuuls don't know of it, and nobody has seen it since.
