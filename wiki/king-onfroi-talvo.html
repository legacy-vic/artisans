    The King of Talvar, seventy-four years old, who holds court in Trion beside his wife, Queen Kota Talvo. His realm is failing. Nirva has closed Blackrock Bay for nearly four decades, resentment festers among his people, and the High King, Alfor Zuul, has spent years dismissing his pleas for aid. Of his six children, only the youngest, Koto, is left for him to protect. He's come to suspect that what became of the others was no accident, and is quietly looking for whoever's behind it while he plays the part of a stable ruler.

    Appearance & Character

    Onfroi has straight, brown, shoulder-length hair and cyan eyes. He's a dutiful and diplomatic ruler, but emotionally distant, and trusts few. He's stricken with guilt over the deaths and disappearances of his children, and must appear stable all the same.

    History

    Early life

    The son of King Cedric Talvo and Lady Haileen Ermsburry, Onfroi survived The Massacre at Wietmoire as a small child, hidden by the servants in the gardens, and was raised across the sea, apart from his mother, who was kept in Icos.

    The crown

    He was fifteen when he traveled to Karlaine with his father, through a winter that sent him into coughing fits he tried to hide. He entered the hall as the heir of an unrecognized ruler, and Aethel Zuul made him a king. See The Crowning of Onfroi. Cedric then took him to see his mother, whom he hadn't met in a long while, and the three of them sailed to Trion. Until he came of age, Cedric ran the realm from beside his throne, and knelt to him in court like anyone else. The night his father stepped back, he told Onfroi the way to High Sonham. Of what was done there, he only ever told him that his grandfather hadn't knelt.

    King of Talvar

    He married Kota, the granddaughter of Lord Hiro Kuroshi. For nearly four decades, Nirva has crippled trade and travel across Blackrock Bay, and Tybal Zuul, whom he'd befriended by letter and invited to Trion, was lost to the creature on the way. It's the first death that Onfroi ever blamed himself for. Cedric told him to ask Aethel for help, and Onfroi never did. He couldn't write to a man for ships when that man's son had died coming to see him. He let his father believe that he had, and Cedric died sure it'd come. By the time Onfroi did write, it was to Alfor Zuul. He's spent years appealing to him for aid, only to be dismissed. At the Royal Conclave, Alfor made a jest of it to Onfroi's face. Onfroi hasn't asked in quite the same way since. Each year, on the way there, he spends a night at Endletonn with the Ermsburrys, because his mother asked it of him before she died. They give him her room, and he's never asked whether they'd really have hanged. At Wietmoire, he's the guest of Alfor's sister, Aeleina Zuul, who keeps the manor, and has become her friend. One year, she told him that her father had waited for a letter, and would've sent everything he had. She didn't know what she was telling him, and he walked in the gardens until morning. Resentment festers among his people. He knows that Kota pays for Novon's winter grain in secret, and has the treasury round the numbers off. Neither has ever said a word about it to the other.

    Story

    Spoiler: Episode 2

    He and Kota came home from nearly a month away with the other sovereigns to find that Koto had brought Artemis and Azlan to the palace on behalf of Novon. He wouldn't hear of it at the table. He remembered the ranch of Azlan's father, and pressed Artemis about his past until the young man told him that he feasted while his people starved. Onfroi answered that a king can't simply speak and have the land obey, and asked what he'd have him do. "Anything," Artemis said. It shook him. He gave the two a shipment to take to Sulala and back, untouched, and promised that if they did, they'd speak again of Novon.

    Spoiler: Episode 4

    The shipment he sent turned out to be crates and sacks of sand.
