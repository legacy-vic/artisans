## S1:E6 - Among Thieves
Status
Not started
Completion
0
Summary
Azra’s episode. We view the world through her perspective, gaining a greater understanding of her character and those around her. The audience learns about her history with both Tibs and Artemis, what motivates her past and present actions, and a potential future arc for her character.
Act I
OPEN: Azra wakes up from the Cerulean Dream. We catch glimpses of her dream: She and Artemis, in a field of blue reeds, silent, peaceful — a feeling not so dissimilar to death, one would assume. Or an eternity in some afterlife.
She treats it as an omen. She’s calm, haunted, focused. Her morning routine is hyper-disciplined. She even clutches an old trinket Artemis gifted many years ago to ground herself.
After readying herself for the day, Azra looks out over the camp and sees the gang preparing, joking, bickering. She’s completely still. Apart from them. A contrast to her character in Episode 1, when she was much more of a social butterfly. The spark that was once there is gone.
Act II
Tibs gives his opening speech to the gang about a heist. He pitches it as a life-changing score. One to settle each and every one of their debts, and one that is sure to put them in the same conversations as the more notorious gangs/syndicates.
Azra sees the spark of ambition. But there’s something… off. He doesn’t look excited. He looks strained. He mentions he will be joining them for this, boosting morale among his people, but this catches Azra’s attention.
Tibs: “Now, I can’t let you have all the fun. That’s why I will be leading point on this one.”
She confronts him about it after the speech in his quarters, and he finally reveals that he has to fill in for Artemis since she failed to bring him in when he asked. She defends herself halfheartedly and urges Tibs to reconsider on account of his time away from ‘the action.’
Tibs: “Oh, quiet. I taught the boy everything he knows; the least you can do is have faith in me.“
Azra: “We don’t need him to make this work.”
Tibs: “No. But it would’ve been perfect. You ever taste perfect, Azra?”
Act III
The Job Begins. Azra is teamed up with a handful of thieves and cutthroats. A bad crew, but one she isn’t unfamiliar with. Already, she feels she may have to carry their weight. Typical.
One is a rival who thinks Azra only rose in the ranks because Tibs favored her.
Another is excessively loyal to Tibs — a fanatic who might get in Azra’s way.
And lastly, a young boy who looks up to Azra. Clearly another orphan taken in by Tibs. Azra can’t help but feel sympathy for the boy, seeing things in him that mirror her own beginnings. In spite of that, Azra is cold toward him — another loyalty she doesn’t want.
The job starts smoothly, but falls apart midway:
Someone does a sloppy job with their task, and alarms are sounded as they’re neck-deep in hostile territory.
Tibs loses his temper more than once — barks orders, near-violent frustration.
At one point, someone dies — someone Tibs claimed would be fine.
Azra saves the young boy, and they escape, bloodied, traumatized.
The kid sees Azra frantically patting herself down until she finds the trinket established earlier. A sigh of relief.
Azra confronts Tibs (tense whisper; sarcastic): “You’re acting like this was supposed to go smooth.”
Tibs (snapping in anger): “It would have! If you had done what you were told and brought my boy back!”
The mask slips. He's not angry about the job. He's upset about Artemis. He no longer has his favorite tool, and Azra is to blame.
Act IV
Azra and the boy reach the rendezvous, injured and exhausted. Tibs isn’t surprised — barely even acknowledges her. After offering the survivors words of affirmation, he calmly informs Azra that the law is onto them, and they know where they’re holed up. A sharp pang is felt in Azra’s chest as Tibs turns to the rest of the gang. He continues giving a speech to the group about how they will all make it, and how those who were lost to them will return. They sit in silence, feeling the noose around their necks tighten, before Tibs breaks the silence with one last statement:
Tibs shakes his head, feeling the weight of this crushing defeat. Made even worse by not having the one person he hinged this mission on.
Tibs: “That stubborn kid damned us all…”
A few people dip their heads, turning away as if they’ve heard this same tirade a million times. Azra, eyes closed, sighs and shakes her head slowly.
Azra (exhausted): “He didn’t give the order, Tibs. You did.”
Tibs: “You had your chance to bring him back, and now look at what it's cost…“
Azra: “You don’t want him back. You want him obedient… And we both know that’s not who he is.“
Tibs doesn’t respond immediately. He can’t. Instead, he looks around at the many faces that depend on him. Those whom he saved from what little of a life they had before… before him. Feeling a sense of vindication, Tibs turns back to Azra, but before he can speak, she turns and walks away. His voice cuts through the misty air, asking where she thinks she’s going. She pauses, doesn’t turn to face him, but states:
Azra: “They’re coming for all of us. So why don’t you focus on who’s actually here for you… and let him walk free.“
Tibs (to himself): “Let him… walk free?“
That’s when it clicks for Tibs. She let him go. Or worse — she wanted him to fail. His paranoia, already bubbling, becomes a full conspiracy.
Act V
The following morning, the camp has largely been packed. The bandits and thieves that remain are being directed by Tibs as he instructs them where to carry what. Anything to feel some sense of control after such a blunder. The young boy that Azra saved enters her tent and notices the mess everywhere.
Kid: “You’re barely packed... [the boy steps closer, noticing the trinket she’s holding] Whatcha lookin’ at?”
Azra: “A gift.”
Kid: “Gift? From who?”
Azra: “…From someone I let down.”
Their quiet moment is interrupted by screams. They rush out to find one of their friends lying dead in the middle of camp, with their body being dragged away. Azra asks a follower what happened, and he explains, condescendingly, that this is what happens to dishonest and disloyal people.
Bandit: “Oh, and your friend, Artemis, will be getting his soon. No loose ends, right?“
She turns around to face Tibs, who is already prepared to meet her gaze. Her grip on the trinket tightens. She doesn’t say a word. Her loyalty has died. She’s made her choice. She just hasn’t acted on it yet.


## S1:E8 - Consequences of Conviction
Status
Not started
Completion
0
Summary
Imprisoned in Trion's gaol after their arrest, Artemis and Azlan must reconcile their differences and work together to devise an escape. There, they encounter Tibs, who attempts to manipulate them into helping him escape as well, though Artemis recognizes his tactics and secretly intends to leave him behind. Meanwhile, Aila, imprisoned alongside them after having previously been their captive, looks out only for herself, while Artemis attempts to earn her trust.
At Trion Palace, Koto struggles with guilt over Artemis and Azlan's imprisonment and attempts to convince her father, King Onfroi, that their arrest was unjust. Tanzu, furious with Koto for allowing events to reach this point, instead takes matters into her own hands and breaks into the gaol to free them. Koto follows in an attempt to stop her, only to become involved in the escape herself.
After freeing Artemis and Azlan, the group refuses to release Tibs. Rather than being surprised, Tibs accepts their betrayal with a chuckle and expresses his pride in Artemis for finally refusing to be manipulated by him. As a final act of aid, Tibs reveals a hidden passage leading to the abandoned Trion shipyard. Artemis silently accepts the information, frees Aila, and the group escapes Trion by sea, leaving Tibs behind.
Act I
Following their arrest, Artemis and Azlan are imprisoned in Trion’s gaol, where the consequences of their increasingly reckless choices finally catch up with them. Their imprisonment also forces the duo to confront their deteriorating relationship. Azlan grows impatient with Artemis for repeatedly putting everyone's lives at risk, while Artemis resists taking responsibility for the damage he has caused. Nevertheless, with no one else coming to rescue them, the two are forced to put their differences aside and begin working together on an escape.
Among the prisoners is Tibs, whom Artemis recognizes, and warns Azlan not to trust a word that comes out of his mouth. Tibs immediately attempts to insert himself into their plans, presenting himself as someone who can help them survive the gaol while subtly manipulating Artemis into feeling responsible for him.
Tibs frames his behavior as concern for Artemis, continuing the paternalistic relationship he has cultivated. Artemis, however, has begun to recognize the pattern. Rather than openly confronting Tibs, he plays along while secretly intending to escape without him.
This shift underscores their relationship. Artemis understands that Tibs's affection and guidance have always come with strings attached.
Aila, meanwhile, remains imprisoned after her earlier captivity at the hands of Artemis and Azlan. Unlike Tibs, she makes no attempt to form alliances for anyone else's benefit. Her priority is simply to survive and escape. Artemis nevertheless tries to build some trust with her, recognizing that Aila could become a valuable ally if they manage to get out.
Their interactions remain tense, with Aila skeptical of Artemis's motives and unwilling to risk herself for people she does not trust.
Act II
Within Trion Palace, Koto is increasingly consumed by guilt. She knows Artemis and Azlan's imprisonment is her fault and tries to convince her father their arrest was unjust.
Onfroi refuses to reconsider, leaving Koto trapped between her obligations as a princess and her growing realization that obedience to her family does not necessarily mean doing what is right.
Tanzu, meanwhile, has little patience for Koto's deliberation. She decides that waiting for permission is pointless and takes matters into her own hands. She infiltrates the gaol and begins freeing the prisoners herself.
Act III
Artemis and Azlan get into trouble within the gaol, sparking conflicts with several prisoners.
Aila recognizes a familiar face among them, and aids Artemis and Azlan in defeating their new rival.
Tanzu infiltrates the gaol and begins freeing the prisoners herself. Koto, who has been following closely, tries to stop Tanzu before she makes things worse. However, in her attempts, Koto is directly drawn into the escape. As the prison descends into chaos, Artemis and Azlan are freed, and Aila eventually becomes part of the group as well.


## S1:E10 - Joint Efforts
Status
Not started
Completion
0
Summary
With Suran inaccessible and their resources dwindling, the group establishes themselves in a bustling shanty district outside the city walls. They split up to earn enough money for food, clothing, and equipment. Koto and Aila find work at a textile shop alongside Tanzu, though their clashing attitudes quickly turn the job into another battlefield. Their argument over privilege, poverty, and whether Koto has ever truly earned anything forces both women to confront how differently they view the world and their own circumstances.
Meanwhile, Artemis and Azlan seek work at the hunters' lodge. Artemis' recklessness initially threatens to get them turned away, but Azra unexpectedly intervenes, supplying them with equipment and offering to take them on a hunt. The uneasy trio investigates reports of a mysterious flying predator attacking livestock and people in the surrounding desert. After discovering its lair, Artemis provokes the creature and nearly gets himself killed. The group follows it underground, where they become trapped within an ancient subterranean complex. Their exploration reveals dead sacred yris and the remains of the creature's previous victims, forcing them deeper into the ruins while Azlan becomes increasingly convinced they are walking toward their deaths.
While Artemis, Azlan, and Azra struggle to survive underground, Tanzu discovers that Koto has gone looking for the imprisoned prince of Suran. Koto, Aila, and Tanzu infiltrate the city through its old canal system, forcing their way through its defenses and learning that the royal family is being held in a chapel-turned-prison. Tanzu repeatedly challenges Koto's motives, accusing her of trying to prove something rather than taking responsibility for the consequences of her actions. Their escape becomes increasingly dangerous when they encounter the city's predators, and Tanzu nearly dies protecting Koto. She is eventually rescued and treated by a local tinkerer, but her injury leaves her incapacitated.
Koto, Aila, and Tanzu ultimately reach the prison and discover the horrifying condition of Queen Ai and Prince Amersi. With Niles and Cho's help, they free the royal family, only to find Suran collapsing into chaos around them. Explosions rip through the city as they flee, and Queen Ai is seriously wounded in the escape. Amersi is devastated when the civilians outside the city refuse to help his family, forcing him to confront the consequences of his family's rule. Koto reassures him while Niles and Cho tend to the queen.
The episode ends with the two halves of the group still separated but moving toward the same destination. Artemis, Azlan, and Azra escape the underground ruins and prepare to continue toward Suran, while Koto and the others flee the burning city with the surviving members of Suran's royal family. The group's disparate objectives—earning money, hunting the mysterious creature, and rescuing the imprisoned royals—ultimately converge into one larger effort.
